# Central-binomial product finiteness

For a positive integer $r$, write $C(r)=\binom{2r}{r}$.

Consider identities

\[
  \prod_{i=1}^{s} C(m_i)=\prod_{j=1}^{t} C(n_j),
\]

where $s,t\geq 1$, every index is a positive integer, and all indices
$m_1,\ldots,m_s,n_1,\ldots,n_t$ are pairwise distinct.

## Exact target

Determine whether only finitely many such identities exist. A negative answer
must give an explicit infinite family and a self-contained proof that, for
every permitted parameter, all indices are positive and pairwise distinct,
the displayed products are exactly equal, and infinitely many different
identities result.

A finite list of numerical coincidences, a search bound, or a proof that omits
distinctness or infinitude does not close the endpoint.
