# Factorial divisibility with logarithmic excess

All logarithms are natural. Divisibility is in the positive integers.

## Exact target

Prove that for every choice of real constants $0<C_1<C_2$ and
$0<\varepsilon<1/2$, there are infinitely many triples of positive integers
$(a,b,n)$, with $a+b\geq n$, such that

\[
  \varepsilon n\leq a,b\leq(1-\varepsilon)n,
\]

and

\[
  a!b!\mid n!(a+b-n)!
\]

and

\[
  C_1\log n<a+b-n<C_2\log n.
\]

The endpoint requires one uniform construction or existence proof for every
fixed triple $C_1,C_2,\varepsilon$, proof of the balance inequalities,
divisibility at every prime, both strict window bounds, and infinitude. A
single triple, one fixed window, an unbalanced tautological construction, or
an unproved appeal to random digits does not close the endpoint.
